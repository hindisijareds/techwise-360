1. Executive Summary & The Dual-Device Challenge

In the TechWise 360 ecosystem, learning is divided into two synergistic environments: the Web-Based Learning Management Portal (used on desktop computers for lessons, interactive guides, quizzes, and analytics) and the Meta Quest 2 Virtual Reality Simulation (used for immersive 6DoF PC hardware assembly and disassembly).

A primary architectural challenge is Single Sign-On (SSO) and Session Handshake. The desktop PC browser and the Meta Quest 2 headset are two physically separate computing devices on different hardware platforms. A student logged into their browser cannot automatically pass their browser's local memory (localStorage) to the standalone Quest 2 headset without an orchestrated server bridge.

3. Offline Mode & Store-and-Forward Synchronization

In Philippine TVL-ICT public school laboratories, internet connectivity can be intermittent or completely unavailable during scheduled class periods. TechWise 360 incorporates a Store-and-Forward Offline Architecture to guarantee zero instructional interruption.

3.1 Local Offline Execution Cycle

  1. Offline Fallback Detection: At boot, the Unity VR client pings the local gateway and Supabase API. If no network response is received within 3 seconds, it switches to OFFLINE_MODE.
  2. Local Roster Verification: The headset utilizes cached class rosters previously synchronized during online charging or teacher setup.
  3. Local Attempt Record: When the learner completes the simulation, performance metrics (time, accuracy, mistakes, component sequence) are packaged into an immutable JSON ledger entry stored in the headset's persistent internal storage (Application.persistentDataPath).
  4. Reconnection Auto-Flush: A background daemon in Unity polls network liveness. When internet connectivity is restored, all attempts flagged with synced: false are dispatched via HTTPS POST to /api/student/vr-attempts, validated server-side, and marked synced: true.

4. Adversarial Threat Model & Security Countermeasures

A rigorous defense panel evaluates whether students can game the system, sabotage peers, or dispute their bad scores. We have designed a multi-layer defense matrix addressing both external and internal threats.

4.1 Threat Scenario 1: Identity Spoofing / Peer Sabotage in Offline Mode

The Threat: Student B enters Student A's (Jared's) Student ID on an offline headset and intentionally fails the simulation (or records 50 mistakes) to ruin Jared's grade and leaderboard standing.
  • Countermeasure 1 — Highest Score Retention Policy: In TechWise 360, a learner's official competency grade and leaderboard position only update when a new attempt achieves a higher competency score. Lower scores never overwrite or reduce an existing passing score.
  • Countermeasure 2 — Secret 4-Digit Offline PIN: Student IDs are public, but personal PINs are private. The headset requires both the Student ID and a private 4-digit PIN (hashed locally against the cached roster). Student B cannot log in as Student A without the secret PIN.
  • Countermeasure 3 — Station & Device Fingerprinting: Every offline record logs the physical headset serial number (device_id) and physical station name. If Student A is assigned to Desk 1 but an attempt originates from Desk 4, an anomaly alert is raised.

4.2 Threat Scenario 2: The "False Sabotage / Cheating" Repudiation Attack

The Threat: Student A plays 10 times, performs poorly with multiple resets, and finally gets a high score on run #10. Student A then lies to the teacher, claiming: "Teacher, someone else used my account for the first 9 runs! Please delete those bad attempts so I have 1 attempt and 0 mistakes on the leaderboard!"

To eliminate this exploit, TechWise 360 implements the following verification mechanisms:

Verification Mechanism Technical Data Captured Forensic Value / Evidence
Hardware Proximity Sensor Quest 2 IR proximity sensor mount/unmount event timestamps. Proves whether the headset was continuously worn on the exact same face across runs #1 through #10 without ever being removed.
Continuous Session Timeline Inter-attempt interval delta (e.g. 30 seconds between attempts). Proves that no second person physically had time to swap headsets inside a monitored computer lab.
Immutable Audit Trail Attempts marked disputed are never deleted from Supabase. Preserves full historical proof of all attempts, scores, and timestamps.
Supervised Re-Test Policy Institutional policy requiring live, in-person re-testing. If a student claims fraud, they must perform the simulation in front of the instructor. Honest students succeed; liars fail to reproduce lucky scores.

5. Pre-Defense Presentation Cheat Sheet & Panel Q&A

Question 1: "How does the student automatically log in to the Meta Quest 2 if they already logged in on the web portal?"
Your Answer:
"Because the Meta Quest 2 and the PC operate on separate physical hardware, our system uses a cloud-synchronized **Station Pairing Handshake**. The student selects their assigned lab headset station on their dashboard. The headset, which continuously polls our Supabase/Cloudflare backend, receives the active student session and transitions directly into the 3D simulation without requiring tedious virtual keyboard typing."
Question 2: "What happens if our school internet connection drops while students are doing VR assembly?"
Your Answer:
"TechWise 360 implements a **Store-and-Forward Offline Architecture**. The headset operates autonomously using cached section rosters, evaluates component assemblies locally, and records performance metrics to encrypted internal storage. Once the headset re-establishes internet connectivity, pending records are automatically batch-uploaded to Supabase."
Question 3: "What stops a student from maliciously logging in as another student in offline mode to ruin their grades or leaderboard standing?"
Your Answer:
"We employ a three-layer defense: First, offline access requires a private 4-digit PIN in addition to the Student ID. Second, our grading algorithm enforces a **Best-Score Evaluation Policy**, meaning a lower score can never overwrite or reduce an established grade. Third, hardware proximity sensor telemetry and physical headset IDs are permanently bound to every attempt, enabling instructors to audit and invalidate fraudulent records."
Question 4: "What if a student lies and claims someone else caused their failed attempts just to improve their leaderboard ranking?"
Your Answer:
"The Meta Quest 2 hardware proximity sensor logs whether the headset was continuously mounted to a user's face across consecutive attempts. If attempts occur back-to-back without headset removal, forensic evidence confirms it was the same student. Furthermore, our institutional policy requires students requesting an attempt reset to perform a proctored, in-person re-test in front of the teacher."
